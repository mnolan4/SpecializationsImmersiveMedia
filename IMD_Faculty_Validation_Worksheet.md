# IMD Faculty Validation Worksheet

**Companion to the Current-State Curriculum Map**  
**Faculty Working Draft — September 2026**

## What we are asking you to do

Complete the sheet for each IMDM course you have taught. Describe the offerings named at the top of that sheet: what every student was required to do in order to finish.

Return those sheets to the Specializations Committee. Stop there.

Do not fill sheets for courses you have not taught. Do not fill the course-coverage table, the partner-course table, or the program synthesis. Those are committee pages.

If two instructors teach the same course, each completes a separate sheet for their own offerings. Do not merge your account with a colleague's.

One sheet may cover more than one semester only when the requirements were the same. If a later offering changed a required assignment, a rating, or a facility every student uses, use a second sheet or write **Varies** and state what changed.

## What counts as an answer

Report requirements, not hopes. An answer names what a student had to do to finish the offering you taught.

These are not answers:

- The catalog description
- What you intend to change next time
- What the strongest project did
- What students could do if they chose an optional tool, facility, or topic

Use these words when they apply. Each one is a complete answer.

| Write this | When |
|---|---|
| **None** | This course does not do the thing asked. |
| **I do not know** | You would be describing someone else's course, or a student path you did not teach. |
| **Varies** | Your own offerings differ. State the difference. |
| **Project-dependent** | Only some students do this, because the project determines it. |

A blank line is an unfinished sheet. **None** is finished.

## What you are not deciding

You are not being asked to approve the current-state map, propose courses, or judge the program. The paragraph under each course title is the committee's reading of published materials. Do not treat it as the answer. Compare your sheet with it only in the last line.

## How to answer items 1–10

### 1–2. Competencies and evidence

In **Your rating**, write the level every student must reach to finish: **I**, **P**, **A**, **D**, **Not taught**, or **Varies**.

| Level | Use it when |
|---|---|
| **I: Introduced** | Every student encounters the concept, tool, vocabulary, or method. |
| **P: Practiced** | Every student applies it more than once, with guidance and feedback. |
| **A: Advanced** | Every student uses it with greater independence, complexity, integration, or specialization. |
| **D: Demonstrated** | Every student synthesizes it in a substantial public or professional presentation. |
| **Not taught** | You do not require it. |
| **Varies** | Your offerings do not share one level. |

If the published map says "when the project is central" and only some projects reach that level, **Your rating** is the level you hold every student to. Put **Project-dependent** in the last column.

In **Required evidence**, name the assignment another faculty member could find on the syllabus. Include the name and when it falls ("Project 2, collaborative installation, weeks 6–10"). If the rating is **Not taught**, write **None**.

In the last column, write **Yes** or **No**. **Yes** means the draft level is the level every student must reach. **No** means you state the difference in a few words.

The rows already list competencies the published map associates with the course. Rate each of them. Add a row only for a competency you require that is not listed.

### 3. Incoming expectations

List skills you rely on in the first weeks, one per line. Count a skill only if students who lack it cannot do the first required work.

Catalog prerequisites count only when that preparation is actually present. If a prerequisite does not show up as a usable skill, list the skill under missing preparation.

If you rely on nothing beyond enrollment, write **None**. If nothing is missing, write **None** there too.

### 4. What students leave with

List what every student must be able to do, or must turn in, to finish. One item per line. Leave out work that only some projects produce.

### 5. Later courses

Name a later course only if a required assignment in your course is made for that course to use, and you have agreed on that handoff with its instructor. Say what is handed off.

If you have not agreed on a handoff, write **None**. Do not list a later course you hope will use the work.

### 6. Continuing earlier work

Check one box.

- **Encouraged.** You ask students to bring earlier work, and you grade the new learning.
- **Permitted.** You allow it when a student asks, and you do not ask for it.
- **Discouraged.** You require work that starts in this course.
- **Not applicable.** Students have no earlier IMD work to bring.

Then write the sentence you say to a student who asks to continue an earlier project. If you checked **Not applicable**, write **None**.

### 7. Facilities, technologies, and equipment

Write one of these three words on every row.

- **Guaranteed.** A required activity uses it. Every student who finishes has used it.
- **Available.** A student or team may use it. Some finish without using it.
- **Not used.** This offering does not use it.

A blank row is unfinished. Add a row for software, hardware, or a platform that is **Guaranteed**. In the next column, **Guided** means instruction or staff runs it. **Independent** means students operate it themselves as part of required work.

### 8. Portfolio-ready documentation

Portfolio-ready means a record the student could show outside the course without you there to explain it: what the work is, what they did, and a viewable file, page, or statement.

Check one box. If you check **Required** or **Optional**, name the assignment. If you check **Not produced**, write **None**.

### 9. Professional roles, practices, or career contexts

Check **None**, or name each role, practice, or career context you introduce. Label each one:

- **Graded requirement.** Finishing the course depends on it.
- **Taught topic.** You teach it, and it is not separately graded.
- **Project-dependent.** It arises only for some projects.

Examples, and only if this offering introduces them: a production role such as designer, artist, programmer, technical director, or producer; collaborative expectations; an asset or production pipeline; client or partner work; budgeting; intellectual property; entrepreneurship; exhibition; promotion or public presentation; portfolio, job, or graduate-study materials; career research; criticism, curation, or research practice.

### 10. Breaks you actually hit

A break is a place where students entering this course are out of step with a required earlier course. Name the skill. Choose one kind of break. Name the earlier course and the assignment in this course where you hit it. If you did not hit one, write **None observed**. If this course has no required earlier course, write **None observed**.

- **Repetition.** You grade students again on a skill a required earlier course already graded, and this course adds no new demand. Example: the first project grades the same asset-management task a prior studio already required.
- **Missing preparation.** You have to teach a skill you counted on a required earlier course to provide, because students arrive without it. Example: you expected the prerequisite to leave students able to structure a program, and you have to teach that before they can start.
- **Abrupt jump.** You require a skill, or a scale of work, that no required earlier course prepared. Example: the first project asks for a room-scale installation, and no required prior course had students work at that scale.

### Last line

The published-map claim on the sheet is the committee's reading of published materials. On a course sheet it is the gray paragraph under the title. It states the job of the course, who it is for, and the competency levels it names. It is not a question.

This line is where you say whether that claim is true of the offerings you taught. Write **Matches** if a colleague could treat the paragraph as an accurate account of what every student had to do.

If it is not accurate, write one sentence. Name the part of the paragraph that is wrong, and name what the offering actually required. Example: "The paragraph says Interaction Design is only Introduced; every student is graded on two interactive studies, which is Practiced."

Do not repeat answers you already wrote above. Do not propose a replacement paragraph.
